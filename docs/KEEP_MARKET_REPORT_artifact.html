Keep Market Calibration

What garage-condo campuses actually sold for, what predicts price and sales speed, and what that means for our sites. Built from county deed records across 26 campuses and 4 operator families.

Bottom line

  • Price follows nearby wealth depth: the share of homes worth $1M+ within 20 minutes is the strongest predictor. Median home value and income barely predict it.
  • Sales speed falls with distance from wealth. In Texas, one operator's campus 20 minutes from wealth sold out in 5 months; the one 64 minutes away still has 57 of 99 units after about 3 years.
  • Unit size isn't a reliable price lever. Price per SF falls, stays flat or rises with size depending on the campus; location and finish matter far more. The unit mix is mainly a speed and risk dial.
  • The contractor buyer is barely visible in deeds (3–4% of LuxeLocker units). That is a floor: tradesmen buying in their own name don't show up.

Evidence behind the conclusions

Owners make money on resaleToy Barn 2018–22 campuses resell at 1.5–2.2× the launch price; Scottsdale resales reach $667–681/SF. LuxeLocker ranges from +70% (Lake Havasu) and +50% (Spanish Springs) to flat (Richland) and slightly negative (Prescott).
Most campuses sell fastEvery Toy Barn campus since 2018 hit 50% closed within 3 months of first closing and 90% within 5–13 months. Newer campuses pre-sold 9–21 months before closing.
Who buysToy Barn owners: median age 61–68, 13–35% under 60, 12–24% mail from out of state, 14–57% entity or trust owners. The Chandler-area list is youngest and most business-heavy.
Unit size and priceNo consistent pattern: price per SF falls with size at Motor Enclave ($450 → $326) and Big Door (~$250 → ~$232), is flat at Toy Barn and Luxe Dream, and rises at Motocave ($304 → $352). Location and finish move price far more than unit size.
Where slow sales came fromExecution and oversupply, not demographics: Prescott cleared only through bulk discounts and an affiliate takeout; Mesquite sold 18 of 215 units in 11 months; Richland took 4 years.
Works buyers and flex3–4% of LuxeLocker units went to operating businesses (deeds hide tradesmen buying in their own name). Utah flex condos ask $315–420/SF for 1,000–1,200 SF bays, so works units needn't be the cheap tier.

Which measures line up with price across 26 campuses (rank correlation): $1M+ home share +0.56, $200K-household share +0.48, drive time to wealth −0.45, seasonal-home share +0.44, count of $200K households +0.30, median home value +0.28, contractor counts ≈ 0.

Sources: 2,600+ unit-level deed and appraisal records (LuxeLocker's AZ/NV/WA/ID counties, Maricopa, Pinellas, Palm Beach, Sarasota, Hillsborough, Lake County IL, and 8 Texas appraisal districts), ACS 2022 tract data, IRS SOI, Census business patterns, Zillow county home values and Mapbox drive times.
